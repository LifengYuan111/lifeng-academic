## Research Question

Which watershed and water-quality tools are best suited to specific nutrient fate and transport applications, data conditions, spatial scales, and decision contexts?

## Why It Matters

Nitrogen, phosphorus, and total suspended solids are represented differently across watershed tools. A transparent comparison helps decision-makers, researchers, and watershed managers avoid selecting models that exceed available data or do not match the intended screening, regulatory, restoration, or forecasting application.

## Methods

The comparative technical review evaluated each tool according to:

- Purpose and application context
- Pollutants simulated, including nutrients and sediment
- Spatial and temporal scale
- Ease of use and required expertise
- Input-data requirements
- Calibration and validation capabilities
- Strengths and limitations

## Key Findings

- No single tool is appropriate for every application; selection must reflect project goals, available data and resources, user expertise, and geographic and temporal scope.
- Simple models are effective for screening and planning, while more complex models support regulatory assessment, best-management-practice evaluation, and long-term forecasting.
- Calibration, validation, and uncertainty analysis are essential for credible results.

## My Contribution

Lifeng Yuan contributed to the comparative technical review and synthesis of model capabilities, limitations, data needs, calibration requirements, and application contexts.

## Practical Relevance

The report recommends matching model complexity to specific project needs, improving documentation and inter-model comparison, integrating real-time monitoring, remote sensing, and machine learning, and supporting standardized cross-agency use for regulatory and restoration planning.
